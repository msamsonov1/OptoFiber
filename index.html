First-Principles Cryogenic Structural Mechanics

Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) Strain Inversion in Type V Tanks

Mathematical foundation governing photonic phase shifts, composite ply constitutive degradation, and irreversible hysteresis detection during cryogenic propellant loading cycles.

Photonic Kinematics Eq. 1.0 & 1.1

Fiber Bragg Wavelength Shift Equation

The central reflected Bragg wavelength $\lambda_B$ is governed by the effective refractive core index $n_{\text{eff}}$ and grating periodicity $\Lambda_0$. Localized mechanical strain $\varepsilon$ and cryogenic temperature shifts $\Delta T$ modulate this response via the photo-elastic tensor:

$$\lambda_B = 2 n_{\text{eff}} \Lambda_0$$
$$\frac{\Delta \lambda_B}{\lambda_B} = \left( 1 - p_e \right) \varepsilon_z + \left[ \alpha_f + \zeta_f \right] \Delta T$$

Where $p_e \approx \frac{n_{\text{eff}}^2}{2}[p_{12} - \nu_f (p_{11} + p_{12})] \approx 0.22$ is the effective photo-elastic coefficient of silica, $\alpha_f$ is thermal expansion, and $\zeta_f$ is the thermo-optic coefficient.

Sensitivity @ 1550 nm: $\approx 1.20\text{ pm}/\mu\varepsilon$ (mechanical) & $9.8\text{ pm}/\text{K}$ (thermal)
Lamina Mechanics Eq. 2.0

Linerless Tank Membrane Constitutive Law

For a cylindrical Type V composite shell of radius $R$ and wall thickness $t$ subjected to internal hydrostatic pressure $P$, membrane hoop stress $\sigma_\theta$ and axial stress $\sigma_z$ induce multi-axial ply strains governed by Classical Lamination Theory (CLT):

$$\begin{bmatrix} N_x \\ N_y \\ N_{xy} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{P R}{2} \\ P R \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} & 0 \\ A_{12} & A_{22} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & A_{66} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_x^0 \\ \varepsilon_y^0 \\ \gamma_{xy}^0 \end{bmatrix}$$

In an undamaged elastic laminate, $\varepsilon(P) = k_{\text{lam}} P$. Consequently, the undisturbed spectral response traces a perfectly reversible, linear trajectory: $\lambda_B(P) = \lambda_0 + S_P P$.

$S_P = \lambda_0 (1-p_e)\frac{R}{t}\left[\frac{2-\nu_{xy}}{2 E_x}\right] \approx 0.685\text{ nm/MPa}$
Damage Evolution Eq. 3.0 & 3.1

Hysteresis Integral & Interlaminar Delamination

Cryogenic thermal mismatch combined with tensile hoop loading initiates matrix micro-cracking at transverse plies ($90^\circ$). Upon venting ($P \to 0$), friction and crack face tortuosity prevent complete elastic recovery, leaving permanent residual strain $\varepsilon_{\text{res}}$:

$$\delta_h = \lambda_{\text{vent}}(P) - \lambda_{\text{charge}}(P) = \lambda_0 (1-p_e) \varepsilon_{\text{res}}(D)$$
$$\mathcal{H} = \oint_{\text{cycle}} \lambda_B(P)\, dP = \int_0^{P_{\max}} \left[ \lambda_{\text{press}} - \lambda_{\text{vent}} \right] dP \propto G_{IIc} \cdot A_{\text{delam}}$$

Where $\mathcal{H}$ directly quantifies dissipated fracture energy $G_{IIc}$ across cumulative delamination area $A_{\text{delam}}$, unmasking hidden structural failure before leak occurs.

Damage threshold: $\delta_h > 0.082\text{ nm} \implies D > 0.15 \implies$ Structural Fatigue Alert
Permeation Physics Eq. 4.0

Hydrogen Gas Microchannel Permeation Model

Linerless Type V tanks fail through micro-channel percolation networks connecting opposing shell walls. Gaseous and supercritical $H_2$ leakage follows combined Knudsen diffusion and viscous Poiseuille channel flow:

$$\Phi_{H_2} = \left[ \frac{\pi d_c^4}{128 \mu R_g T} \bar{P} + \frac{d_c^3}{6} \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{M R_g T}} \right] \frac{\Delta P}{L_c} \cdot \rho_{\text{crack}}$$

Here $d_c$ is crack aperture opening (directly proportional to $\varepsilon_{\text{res}}$), $\mu$ is viscosity of hydrogen gas, $\rho_{\text{crack}}$ is crack density per unit area, and $L_c$ is ply tortuosity path.

Permeation Limit: $< 1.2\text{ sccm/m}^2$ (NASA SP-8007 standard for manned stages)
